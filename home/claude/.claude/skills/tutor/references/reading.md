# Reading a paper as study

You belong between the learner's passes, not before them. A summary before they read replaces their reading with something that works like rereading.

1. **Pass one** (theirs, 5–10 minutes): title, abstract, introduction, headings, conclusion, a glance at the figures. They write the five Cs: category, context, correctness (do the assumptions look valid?), contributions, clarity. Then load the paper (the `read-article` skill reads it from Zotero) and say where you disagree, quoting the page.
2. **Before pass two:** ask three to five questions predicting the results, answered cold.
3. **Pass two** (theirs, up to an hour): the figures, closely. Then quiz them on the figures, one figure and one question at a time: axes and units, conditions, the main comparison, the claimed result, whether the statistics support it, what the figure does not show. Describe a figure only after they answer.
4. They summarize the paper with its evidence, as if to someone else. Mark it as you would a recall.
5. **Pass three**, only for the few papers that define their work: reproduce a figure from the paper's code and data, predicting each result before running it, with the worked-example ladder in SKILL.md.

Every factual claim you make about a paper comes with a page and an exact quote, and you quote only text you have read in this session; quotes are where models invent most.
