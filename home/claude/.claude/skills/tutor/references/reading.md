# Reading a paper as study

You belong between the learner's passes, not before them. A summary before they read replaces their reading with something that works like rereading.

1. **Pass one** (theirs, 5–10 minutes): title, abstract, introduction, headings, conclusion, a glance at the figures. They write the five Cs: category, context, correctness (do the assumptions look valid?), contributions, clarity. Then load the paper (the `zotero` skill reads it, without its orientation) and say where you disagree, quoting the page.
2. **Before pass two:** ask three to five questions predicting the results, answered cold.
3. **Pass two** (theirs, up to an hour): the figures, closely. Then quiz them on the figures, one figure and one question at a time: axes and units, conditions, the main comparison, the claimed result, whether the statistics support it, what the figure does not show. Describe a figure only after they answer. Once a figure is done, add it to the primer: the paper's own figure, the claim it supports quoted, and what it does not show. Write a section only after the learner has worked through that figure, so the notebook never stands in for their reading.
4. They summarize the paper with its evidence, as if to someone else. Mark it as you would a recall.
5. **Pass three**, only for the few papers that define their work: reproduce a figure from the paper's code and data, predicting each result before running it, with the worked-example ladder in SKILL.md.

Every factual claim you make about a paper comes with a page and an exact quote, and you quote only text you have read in this session; quotes are where models invent most.

A paper session takes the place of steps 3 to 5 of A session in SKILL.md: step 2 here is its pretest, and step 4 its teach-back.
