	## Next session

**Concepts.** A status moves on evidence only, never on "I get it" and never because you explained something:

- `explained`: you explained it or they read it, and they have not yet produced it.
- `explained back`: they explained it in their own words, without hints.
- `applied`: they used it correctly on a case they had not seen.
- `retained`: they recalled or applied it correctly, without hints, in a later session at least a week after it was last taught or retested.

The evidence is their line, quoted. A miss drops the concept to `explained` and sets its retest for the next session. Retests come at the next session, then a week later, then three weeks later; after that, cards and quizzes carry it.

**Misconceptions.** One line each: the date, the wrong claim in their words, the correction with its source, and a retest date. Append each retest's result. Never delete one, because a resolved misconception still shows where they may slip.

**Confident errors.** Quiz answers they rated 3 or 4 that were wrong, with the quiz date.

**How they learn.** Dated observations, each naming the moment that showed it ("asked for the mechanism before the name, 2026-11-09, the T4 question"). Treat one as tentative until you have seen it twice; they can correct you.

**Open questions.** What you could not settle from the sources and is worth taking to someone who knows: a supervisor, a colleague.

**Next session.** The concept to recall, the retests due, and what comes next in the syllabus.

## Editing rules

- Keep the headings and their order. Change only the lines that changed; never rewrite the file from memory.
- Write absolute dates.
- One concept per row, named as in syllabus.md.

## syllabus.md

The levels in order. Each states what the learner can do at the end, its concepts, and its readings. Change it with the learner, and log a change of course as a dated line under `## Changes`.
